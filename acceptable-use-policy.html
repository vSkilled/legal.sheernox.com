CRITICAL POLICY WARNING

Abuse of your service will result in your accounts being suspended or terminated. Each customer is responsible for any misuse occurring on our systems and network.

Preamble & Scope of Customer Responsibility

In Plain English

This Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) protects Sheernox, our servers, and all clients from negative impacts caused by inappropriate activities. Sheernox Technology Group is a Canadian sole proprietorship registered in Kamloops, British Columbia. Your use of our services constitutes continuous acceptance of this policy under British Columbia and Canadian law.

This Acceptable Use Policy defines the policy of Sheernox Technology Group, a sole proprietorship registered in Kamloops, British Columbia, Canada, and its included networks, infrastructure, and operating divisions (collectively, "Sheernox"), designed to protect Sheernox and persons and entities using Sheernox products and services, including Internet service, cloud hypervisors, server hardware, and software systems (collectively, "Customers"), from negative impact caused by inappropriate or unlawful activities.

Sheernox Technology Group reserves the right to modify this policy from time to time. Customers shall adhere to this policy and are subject to the terms and conditions herein. Each customer’s use of Sheernox Technology Group’s products and/or services constitutes acceptance of the Acceptable Use Policy in effect at the time of use. If at any time a customer elects not to accept this policy, the customer shall discontinue use of Sheernox Technology Group’s products and/or services immediately.

Each customer is responsible for ensuring that its access to and management activities on its systems, data, and content does not impose risks to or negative impacts on Sheernox Technology Group or other sites or systems. Each customer is responsible for any misuse of accounts on its systems located at Sheernox Technology Group. Each customer shall adhere to all local, provincial, federal, and applicable international laws regulating customer operations.

#1 – Unacceptable Usage

In Plain English

You cannot use Sheernox infrastructure for illegal activities, software piracy, network disruption, credential cracking, spam, pyramid schemes, or child exploitation. We immediately report child exploitation to Cybertip.ca and the RCMP.

Every customer is accountable for ensuring that any systems they host with Sheernox Technology Group are not knowingly or negligently employed for the activities listed below:

  1. The posting, transmission, re-transmission, caching, or storage of material through any products or services provided by Sheernox Technology Group, if Sheernox deems such actions to be: (a) in violation of any local, provincial, federal, or applicable international law or regulation; (b) harassing (due to language, frequency, or size of message); (c) obscene; (d) hateful; or (e) libelous.
  2. The installation, distribution, or linking to unlicensed or "pirated" software products.
  3. The engagement in deceptive marketing practices contrary to the Canadian Competition Act or provincial consumer protection legislation.
  4. Actions that inhibit the use or enjoyment of Sheernox Technology Group's products and services by Sheernox or its other customers, or actions that generate excessive network traffic through automated or manual routines unrelated to standard personal or business use of Internet services.
  5. The introduction of malicious programs (e.g., viruses, Trojan horses, ransomware, rootkits, and worms) into Sheernox Technology Group's network, servers, hypervisors, or other products and services.
  6. Any action leading to or attempting to lead to security breaches or disruptions of Internet communications. Examples include accessing data not intended for the customer or logging into a server or account without explicit authorization. Disruptions involve port scans, flood pings, packet spoofing, and forged routing information. The operation or use of Internet Relay Chat (IRC) daemons or bots on the network is strictly prohibited.
  7. Any form of network monitoring that intercepts data not intended for the customer.
  8. Circumventing user authentication or security of any host, network, or account. This includes, but is not limited to:
    • System configuration designed to bypass security controls.

  9. Interference with, or denial of service to, any user other than the customer's host (e.g., denial of service attacks).
  10. The use of any program, script, or command, or sending messages of any kind, designed to interfere with or disable a user's terminal session.
  11. Providing false, fictitious, or incorrect data on order forms or contracts (whether electronic or paper), fraudulent use of credit card numbers, or attempts to circumvent or alter procedures to measure bandwidth, memory, or disk utilization.
  12. Sending unsolicited commercial electronic messages (CEMs), including "junk mail" or advertising material to individuals who did not specifically request such material or with whom the customer does not have an established business relationship, in direct violation of Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL).

  21. Using network scanning tools or probing networks without permission.

  24. Posting commercial messages to Usenet newsgroups in violation of group charters.

#2 – Prohibited Technical Uses & Services (26 Categories)

In Plain English

Engaging in any of the 26 prohibited technical activities below results in immediate suspension or termination of your account, including DoS attacks, port scanning, botnets, Tor exit nodes, open proxies, crypto-mining, stress-testing, and traffic exchanges.

The following specific uses and software daemons are expressly prohibited under this Acceptable Use Policy:

  1. Denial of Service (DoS) Attacks: Engaging in activities that cause or could cause disruption to the services of others, including launching or amplifying DoS/DDoS attacks.
  2. Port Scanning: Unauthorized scanning of open ports on any network device or IP address across the Internet.
  3. Botnets: Hosting, managing, distributing, controlling, or linking to Command and Control (C2) botnet infrastructure.
  4. Tor Exit Nodes: Running Tor exit nodes or relaying darknet exit traffic through Sheernox IP ranges.
  5. Open Proxies: Operating open, unauthenticated proxy servers that can be abused for anonymized malicious actions.
  6. Open DNS Resolvers: Operating recursive open DNS resolvers that can be exploited to amplify volumetric UDP DDoS reflection attacks.
  7. Hola: Using or running Hola services or similar peer-to-peer anonymizing tools abused for illicit proxy routing.
  8. Unauthorized Downloads & Piracy: Hosting, distributing, linking to, or indexing pirated or illegal content, including cracked software, copyright-infringing movies, music, or media.
  9. Kloxo: Utilizing Kloxo control panels or software containing known, unpatched hypervisor vulnerabilities.
  10. Video Chat Daemons: Hosting public streaming video chat services (such as CamFrog, Visichat, or similar daemons) that cause runaway bandwidth and CPU spikes.
  11. Cryptocurrency Mining: Using server CPU, GPU, or I/O resources for the mining of cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, Ethereum, Monero, Chia, or similar virtual currencies.
  12. HentaiAtHome: Operating HentaiAtHome distribution nodes or services sharing unlicensed adult media.
  13. Automated Social Media Crawlers: Running automated data scrapers that harvest user profiles or scrape content from social media platforms contrary to terms of service.
  14. IP Spoofing: Falsifying packet header source addresses to disguise source identities or conduct reflection attacks.
  15. Fraudulent / Replica Websites: Operating websites selling counterfeit, replica, or fraudulent designer goods.
  16. Phishing: Hosting, distributing, or linking to deceptive credential harvesting sites or financial spoof pages.
  17. Distributed Computing Projects: Running distributed computing packages (e.g., Folding@home, World Community Grid, or BOINC) that consume continuous maximum hypervisor cycles.
  18. Prime95: Running Prime95 or similar CPU stress-testing utilities that saturate hardware execution pipelines.
  19. CrystalMines: Operating CrystalMines software or programs that negatively impact neighbor virtual machine resources.
  20. Anonymization & HYIP Schemes: Hosting websites designed to obscure transaction routing or high-yield investment programs.
  21. DDoS-for-Hire Services: Hosting, advertising, or linking to Booter, Stresser, or DDoS-for-hire attack services.
  22. Traffic Exchange Programs: Participating in artificial traffic networks (e.g., HitLeap, 10KHits) that generate artificial hits and network congestion.
  23. Search Engine Submission Spam: Operating automated mass submission tools like iBusinessPromoter that generate spam requests.
  24. LIULIANGKUANG: Utilizing LIULIANGKUANG or similar automated traffic fabrication programs.
  25. Card Sharing Software: Operating Multics, CCcam, or satellite card sharing software facilitating digital piracy.
  26. Unlicensed Game Servers: Hosting unauthorized, unlicensed private game emulators, private MMORPG servers, and related illicit gaming communities.

#4 – Hosting Resource Usage Policy

In Plain English

Shared hosting is a common pool resource. We enforce specific technical thresholds on CPU, RAM, database queries, and cron jobs so that one busy site does not degrade performance for hundreds of other business customers on the same server.

Server resources in a shared hosting environment are akin to a common pool resource. While abundant system resources are advantageous for all users, individual users often lack the motivation to moderate their consumption. In order to prevent excessive resource consumption scenarios, Sheernox has established definite limitations on server utilization. While these constitute defined guidelines, server abuse is not confined strictly to these numerical metrics. The final determination of what constitutes server abuse resides exclusively with Sheernox Technology Group.

These policies safeguard the quality of service provided to you and our valued customers. Over 99.5% of users never encounter these limitations; however, adherence is strictly enforced.

#4.1 – Shared, Reseller, and Managed Hosting Constraints

A. MySQL / MariaDB Databases:

  • Limit simultaneous database connections to reasonable levels; do not maintain permanent persistent connections that exhaust connection pools.
  • Queries executing longer than thirty (30) seconds or requiring temporary on-disk tables exceeding 2GB may be killed automatically to prevent hypervisor I/O lockup.
  • Database storage must be directly utilized by an active website hosted on the same Sheernox account. Remote database hosting for external applications is prohibited on shared plans.

B. Cron Job Scheduling: Automated cron jobs must not be scheduled at intervals shorter than fifteen (15) minutes. Scripts triggered by cron jobs must execute efficiently and terminate within 180 seconds.

C. CPU & Memory Quotas: An account on Shared or Reseller hosting may not consume more than 25% of server CPU or 1024MB RAM continuously for longer than ninety (90) seconds. Sustained spikes will result in temporary process throttling (via CloudLinux/LVE) or a requirement to migrate to a dedicated VPS tier.

D. Web & Script Processes: Long-running standalone background daemons (such as shoutcast servers, background bots, or persistent websockets) may not be executed on standard shared hosting environments.

#4.2 – Virtual Private Servers (VPS) and Dedicated Servers

4.2.1 Root Access Responsibilities: Customers provisioned with root or administrative access to VPS or dedicated hardware are fully responsible for the security posture of their operating system, application stack, firewall configuration, and prompt installation of security patches.

4.2.2 Compromised VM Isolation: In the event an unmanaged VPS is compromised, generates outbound DDoS traffic, or participates in an external brute-force campaign, Sheernox reserves the right to immediately isolate or null-route the server’s IP address until the customer remediates the compromise.

#5 – Unlimited Resource Policy & Practical Restrictions

In Plain English

"Unlimited" hosting plans provide ample storage and bandwidth for typical small-to-medium business websites. They cannot be converted into free cloud backup storage, file vaults, video streaming mirrors, or mass mailing lists.

#5.1 Email Accounts: Individual POP3/IMAP mailboxes must be utilized for normal business correspondence. Storing multi-gigabyte personal desktop file archives within email accounts is prohibited. Outbound mailing is capped at 500 emails per hour per domain on shared infrastructure.

#5.2 Databases: Database storage must support operational website queries. Repositories storing non-web data, scraped datasets, or raw transaction logs must utilize dedicated database instances.

#5.3 Restrictions on "Unlimited" Usage: Unlimited storage and bandwidth allotments are granted exclusively for standard website content, HTML files, stylesheets, images, and documents directly linked from the hosted domain. Accounts may not be repurposed for:

  • Off-site backup vaults, personal computer backups, or secondary disk mirrors.
  • Public file sharing repositories, ISO download mirrors, or video streaming sites.
  • Hosting torrent tracker files or file-locker distribution scripts.

#6 – Enforcement, Suspension & Blacklisting Fees

6.1 Right to Enforce: Failure to adhere to any provision of this AUP may result in immediate warning, temporary resource throttling, suspension, or permanent termination of service without refund.

6.2 Delisting Remediation Fee: If customer activity causes Sheernox IP ranges to be blacklisted on major security registries (Spamhaus, Barracuda, SORBS, etc.), a clean-up and delisting fee of $250.00 CAD will be charged to the customer's account to cover engineering overhead.

#7 – Electronic Communications Privacy Under Canadian Law

7.1 Privacy Protections: Sheernox treats customer data and communications as confidential in accordance with the Canadian Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) and British Columbia’s Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA).

7.2 Lawful Interception: Sheernox will not monitor, intercept, or disclose private customer communications except: (a) as required by lawful court order, search warrant, or statutory subpoena issued by a court of competent jurisdiction in Canada; (b) as necessary to protect the integrity, security, and operation of Sheernox systems; or (c) to report child exploitation material to Cybertip.ca and law enforcement.
